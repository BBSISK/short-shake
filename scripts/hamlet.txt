Let's do Hamlet in two minutes.
Prince Hamlet comes home to Denmark to find his father dead and his mother married to his uncle Claudius, who is now king. Not a great week.
Then his father's ghost appears on the castle wall and says Claudius murdered him. The ghost wants revenge.
Hamlet decides to find out if it's true. He starts acting strangely, so strangely that nobody can tell whether it's an act. He spends a lot of time thinking out loud, including one very famous speech that is mostly just overthinking.
To test Claudius, Hamlet gets a group of actors to perform a play that looks suspiciously like the murder. Claudius watches, panics, and leaves the room. That's good enough for Hamlet.
Then comes the worst moment for Hamlet's plan. He hears a noise behind a curtain in his mother's room, stabs through it, and kills Polonius, the king's advisor. It was the wrong person.
Claudius has had enough. He sends Hamlet to England with a letter ordering his execution. Hamlet finds the letter, swaps it for another, and the people carrying it are the ones in trouble. He sails back to Denmark.
Claudius isn't done. He sets up a fencing match between Hamlet and Laertes, Polonius's son, who wants revenge too. The sword is poisoned. There's also a poisoned drink, just in case.
It goes wrong for everyone. Hamlet's mother drinks the poisoned cup, Laertes and Hamlet wound each other with the poisoned sword, and Hamlet finally kills Claudius. Then Hamlet dies too.
His friend Horatio is left to explain what happened.
The moral: if the plan is revenge, bring a better plan.
