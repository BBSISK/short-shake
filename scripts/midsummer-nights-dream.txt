Let's do A Midsummer Night's Dream in two minutes.
In Athens, Hermia loves Lysander. Her father wants her to marry Demetrius. The Duke says she must obey her father, or become a nun. Hermia and Lysander decide to run away into the forest. Her friend Helena loves Demetrius, who doesn't love her. She tells Demetrius about the plan, hoping it will help. So now all four of them are in the forest.
It's full of fairies. Oberon, the fairy king, is arguing with his queen, Titania. He sends his helper Puck to fetch a flower. Whoever has its juice put on their eyes will fall in love with the first thing they see.
Oberon sees Demetrius being rude to Helena, so he tells Puck to use the flower on him. Puck finds the wrong Athenian. Lysander wakes up, sees Helena, and falls for her. Oberon fixes it by putting the flower on Demetrius too. Now both men love Helena, and Helena thinks they're both making fun of her.
Meanwhile, a group of amateur actors is rehearsing a play for the Duke's wedding. Puck turns one of them, Bottom, into a donkey. Titania wakes up and sees him first. She falls in love with a donkey, and he asks for some hay.
Oberon eventually takes pity on everyone. Titania is released from the spell, Bottom gets his own head back, and Lysander falls in love with Hermia again. Demetrius keeps the potion on purpose. Nobody mentions it.
Everyone wakes up, decides it was a dream, and returns to Athens for three weddings and a very bad play.
It's a play about love making people foolish, and about fairies being no better at it.
The moral: never trust a love potion.
That was A Midsummer Night's Dream. Shake you later.
