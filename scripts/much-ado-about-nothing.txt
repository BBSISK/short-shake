Let's do Much Ado About Nothing in two minutes.
A group of soldiers comes home to Messina, in Sicily. Claudio, one of the young soldiers, falls for Hero, the governor's daughter, and gets his prince to ask on his behalf. The answer is yes.
There is also Beatrice, Hero's cousin, and Benedick, one of the soldiers. They have a long history of insulting each other. When they meet, Beatrice says nobody is listening to him. Benedick calls her Lady Disdain. Each has sworn never to marry.
Their friends decide to fix that. Benedick is made to overhear his friends say Beatrice is secretly in love with him. Beatrice overhears Hero saying the same about Benedick. Both of them fall for it, and, to their own surprise, fall for each other.
Meanwhile Don John, the prince's bitter half-brother, wants to ruin the wedding. With his friend Borachio, he sets up a scene: a woman in Hero's dress at Hero's window at night, with a man calling her Hero. Claudio is watching, and he believes it.
At the wedding, Claudio refuses to marry Hero in front of everyone, and says why. Hero faints. Everyone assumes she's guilty, except Beatrice and the friar, who thinks she's innocent.
The friar has a plan. They say Hero has died, which should make Claudio feel awful, and the truth will come out.
Beatrice and Benedick finally admit they love each other. Beatrice's first request is for him to challenge Claudio. Benedick is surprised, but he agrees.
The truth does come out, thanks to the town watch, a group of well-meaning and very confused officers who overhear Borachio boasting about the plan. Claudio apologises, and agrees to marry Hero's cousin, who turns out to be Hero. And Beatrice and Benedick give in too.
The moral: never trust gossip.
That was Much Ado About Nothing. Shake you later.
