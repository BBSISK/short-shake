Let's do Julius Caesar in two minutes.
Rome, 44 BC. Julius Caesar has won the civil war, and the crowds adore him. A fortune-teller in the street warns him to beware the ides of March. Caesar calls him a dreamer and walks on.
Meanwhile, a senator called Cassius is worried that Caesar is becoming a king. He works on his friend Brutus, who is respected by everyone and loves Caesar, but loves Rome more. Brutus joins the plot.
The conspirators meet in secret. Cassius wants to kill Mark Antony, Caesar's loyal friend, as well. Brutus says no. They are liberators, he says, not murderers. It is a very bad decision.
On the morning of March the fifteenth, Caesar's wife, Calpurnia, begs him to stay home. She has dreamed that he was bleeding. Caesar agrees, until one of the conspirators arrives, and tells him the dream means Rome loves him. Caesar changes his mind, and goes to the Senate.
There the conspirators crowd round him and stab him. Brutus is the last. Caesar says, "And you, Brutus? Then fall, Caesar." And he dies.
Brutus speaks to the crowd and explains that he did it for Rome. They cheer him. Then he lets Antony speak.
Antony begins, "Friends, Romans, countrymen, lend me your ears." He says again and again that Brutus is an honourable man, and each time it sounds less true. Then he reads out Caesar's will, which leaves money to every Roman. The crowd turns. Brutus and Cassius flee the city.
Antony joins forces with Caesar's young heir, Octavius, and a man called Lepidus. Brutus and Cassius raise an army in Greece. The night before the battle at Philippi, Caesar's ghost visits Brutus, and says he will see him there.
He does. At Philippi, the conspirators lose. Even Antony says Brutus was the noblest Roman of them all.
The moral: be careful who you leave alive, and what you let them say.
That was Julius Caesar, and that was every play. Shake you later.
