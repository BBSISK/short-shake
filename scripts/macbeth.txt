Let's do Macbeth in two minutes.
Macbeth is a Scottish general who has just won a battle. On his way home, three witches tell him he will become king. He tells his wife, and she is very interested.
King Duncan comes to stay with them. Lady Macbeth says this is the perfect chance. Macbeth isn't sure, but she keeps pushing. He gives in, and Duncan is killed in the night. Macbeth is crowned.
The trouble is that a king who got the crown this way has to keep looking over his shoulder. The witches also said that the descendants of his friend Banquo will be kings. So Macbeth has Banquo killed. Banquo's son escapes.
At the celebration dinner, Banquo's ghost turns up and sits in Macbeth's seat. Only Macbeth can see it. The guests are confused, and Lady Macbeth makes his excuses.
Macbeth goes back to the witches. They give him three more predictions. Beware Macduff. No man born of a woman can harm him. And he won't be beaten until a forest moves to his castle.
He feels better about all of this. He shouldn't. Macbeth has Macduff's family killed, and Macduff swears revenge. Meanwhile Lady Macbeth is sleepwalking, trying to wash her hands of what they've done.
An English army marches on Macbeth's castle, and each soldier cuts a branch from the trees of Birnam Wood to hide behind. From the castle, it looks like the forest is moving.
Macbeth fights Macduff and says no man born of woman can hurt him. Macduff says he was born by caesarean. Macbeth loses, and Malcolm, Duncan's son, becomes king.
The moral: read the fine print on a prophecy.
