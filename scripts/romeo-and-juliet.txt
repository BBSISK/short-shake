Let's do Romeo and Juliet in two minutes.
In Verona, two families, the Montagues and the Capulets, have been enemies for so long that nobody remembers why.
Romeo is a Montague. He is also in love with a girl called Rosaline, who isn't interested. His friends take him to a party at the Capulets' house in disguise. There he meets Juliet, falls for her instantly, and kisses her. Then they find out who the other one is.
That night Romeo climbs into the Capulet garden. Juliet is on her balcony. They talk for a long time, and by the end of the scene they've decided to get married.
The next day, a priest, Friar Laurence, marries them in secret. He hopes it might end the feud.
It doesn't. Juliet's cousin Tybalt challenges Romeo to a fight. Romeo refuses, so Romeo's friend Mercutio steps in, and Tybalt kills Mercutio. Romeo, furious, kills Tybalt. The prince banishes Romeo from Verona.
Juliet's parents, who know nothing about the marriage, tell her she's going to marry a man called Paris. She goes to the friar for help.
He has a plan. She will take a potion that makes her look dead. Her family will bury her, and Romeo will come and collect her. He writes to Romeo to explain.
The letter never arrives. Romeo hears that Juliet is dead, and nobody tells him it's fake. He goes to her tomb. Both lovers die. The two families finally end the feud, but it's too late.
It's a play about two teenagers, and about how quickly everything can go wrong when adults don't talk to each other.
