Shakespeare, in a hurry

Julius Caesar

A popular leader, a warning he ignores, and the most famous funeral speech in English.

A note: It covers a political assassination and a civil war. Our version keeps the violence off screen, and does not show how the main characters die.

The plot in 30 seconds

Caesar is the most popular man in Rome, and a stranger warns him to beware the ides of March. Cassius persuades Brutus that Caesar is becoming a king, and the conspirators stab Caesar in the Senate. At his funeral, Brutus speaks first and wins the crowd, then Mark Antony speaks and turns it. Antony joins Octavius and Lepidus, and the conspirators lose the battle at Philippi.

Who is who

  • Julius Caesar: Rome's popular leader
  • Brutus: respected senator, Caesar's friend, joins the plot
  • Cassius: the organiser, and the one who starts it
  • Mark Antony: Caesar's loyal friend, and a very good speaker
  • Octavius: Caesar's young heir

Words you already use

"Beware the ides of March"

The Soothsayer to Caesar (Act 1, Scene 2). The ides is the 15th.

"Et tu, Brute?"

Caesar to Brutus as he is stabbed (Act 3, Scene 1).

"Friends, Romans, countrymen, lend me your ears"

Antony begins his funeral speech (Act 3, Scene 2).

Background

Probably written in 1599. It is based on the real events of 44 BC in Rome, and Shakespeare took the story from Plutarch.
