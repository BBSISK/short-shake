Shakespeare, in a hurry

The Merchant of Venice

A friend borrows money, the lender wants a pound of flesh, and a lawyer finds a loophole.

A note: The play contains antisemitic attitudes, and its treatment of Shylock is still debated. Our summary keeps to the legal and money plot and does not make fun of him as a person.

The plot in 30 seconds

Antonio signs a loan with a nasty penalty clause so his friend Bassanio can woo Portia. When the loan goes bad, the case reaches court, where Portia in disguise wins it by reading the contract very literally: a pound of flesh, but no blood.

Who is who

  • Antonio: the merchant who signs without reading
  • Shylock: the lender
  • Bassanio: the friend who needs the money
  • Portia: heiress, and the cleverest person in the play

Words you already use

"All that glisters is not gold"

From the casket scene, where suitors choose between gold, silver and lead. We usually say "glitters" now.

"Love is blind"

Jessica says it in this play. It is older than Shakespeare, though: Chaucer used a version around 1405.

Background

Written in the 1590s. Venice was a trading city, which is why money and contracts drive the plot.
