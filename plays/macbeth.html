Shakespeare, in a hurry

Macbeth

A general is told he will be king. His wife helps him speed things up.

The plot in 30 seconds

Three witches tell Macbeth he will be king. Pushed by Lady Macbeth, he murders King Duncan, then kills more people to protect the crown. Guilt, a sleepless wife, a moving forest and a loophole in a prophecy finish him off.

Who is who

  • Macbeth: general turned king, and not a relaxed one
  • Lady Macbeth: pushes him to do it, then cannot sleep
  • The Witches: give prophecies, never the full details
  • Banquo: Macbeth's friend, who turns up as a ghost at dinner
  • Macduff & Malcolm: lead the army that ends it

Words you already use

"The be-all and the end-all"

Macbeth says it in Act 1, Scene 7, while weighing up the murder of Duncan. Today it means the most important thing.

"What's done is done"

Lady Macbeth says it in Act 3, Scene 2. Shakespeare did not coin it, though: it may echo a proverb from 14th-century French.

Background

Written in the early 1600s and set in medieval Scotland.
